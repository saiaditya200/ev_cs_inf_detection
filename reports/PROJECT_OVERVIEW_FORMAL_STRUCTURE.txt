SECTION 1.0: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROJECT ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Executive Summary
The rapid transition toward electric mobility requires data-driven, equitable, and 
grid-viable deployment of Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment (EVSE). This project, 
titled "Explainable AI Framework for Smart EV Charging Infrastructure Planning," 
delivers a scientifically validated, leakage-controlled, and spatially aware 
decision-support system engineered specifically for the 33 administrative districts 
of Telangana, India.

1.2 Two-Tier Decision Architecture
The framework resolves the conflict between site-level engineering feasibility and 
macro-level policy resource allocation through a two-tier decision hierarchy:
  1. Micro-Level Predictive Intelligence (Station Level - Track A):
     A supervised machine learning classification engine predicts whether a proposed 
     or existing station candidate is best suited for DC Fast Charging (>= 50 kW) 
     or AC Slow / Non-Fast Charging (< 50 kW). The engine relies exclusively on 21 
     certified exogenous spatial, road topology, and demographic indicators accessible 
     prior to station construction, strictly preventing target leakage. Model predictions 
     are fully interpretable via TreeSHAP attributions and actionable counterfactual 
     reasoning.
  2. Macro-Level Strategic Synthesis (Regional Level - Tracks B & C):
     A multi-criteria decision-making layer combines longitudinal EV registration 
     dynamics from the official MoRTH VAHAN registry (Track B) and commercial electric 
     utility throughput from TGSPDCL DISCOM billing records (Track C). Using Analytic 
     Hierarchy Process (AHP) calibrated weighting and the Technique for Order of 
     Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS), it computes the Explainable 
     Infrastructure Priority Score (EIPS) to rank all 33 districts for state investment.
  3. Interactive Geospatial Deployment:
     The end-to-end framework is deployed as a production-grade Flask REST API service 
     paired with a responsive, dark glassmorphic web dashboard featuring Leaflet and 
     Folium geospatial GIS mapping.


SECTION 2.0: TWO-STAGE PROJECT EVOLUTION & LITERATURE BENCHMARK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Research Evolution Context
The methodology of this major project evolved across two distinct, sequential phases:

Stage 1: Initial Research Foundation (The Literature Starting Point)
  - Inspiration Source: Published research paper in Electronics Letters (2024), 
    titled "Machine learning-based multivariate forecasting of electric vehicle 
    charging station demand" (DOI: 10.1049/ell2.70104).
  - Scope of Base Paper: Focused on temporal multivariate forecasting of continuous 
    charging energy demand (kWh) across individual time steps using various deep 
    learning models (RNN, LSTM, GRU, CNN-LSTM), reporting Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) 
    as the best forecaster (RMSE ~ 0.00297, MAE ~ 0.00266).
  - Initial Project Plan: Attempted to replicate time-series demand forecasting using 
    synthetic datasets (such as UrbanEV) and linear regression assumptions.

Stage 2: Subsequent Research Discussion & Implemented Methodology (Active System)
  - Critical Feasibility Audit: In-depth analysis of Indian regional governance data 
    revealed that physical, continuous, multi-year station-level kWh transaction 
    logs do not exist in public domain records for Telangana.
  - The Circularity Trap: Synthesizing fake station-level demand by combining road 
    data and demographics—and subsequently training machine learning models on those 
    same features—creates severe mathematical circularity and synthetic data leakage.
  - Methodological Resolution: The core machine learning task was reformulated around 
    an observable, verifiable ground-truth target: classifying physical station 
    infrastructure tiers (DC Fast vs AC Slow) using strictly exogenous site variables. 
    Regional EV registration growth and utility grid context were preserved at their 
    authentic administrative grains and integrated downstream via Multi-Criteria 
    Decision Analysis.

SECTION 4.0: MULTI-SOURCE DATA ARCHITECTURE & INTEGRITY PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Master Dataset Composition
The master repository dataset (FINAL_PROJECT_DATASET.csv) integrates verified public 
and institutional data sources across all 33 districts of Telangana:
  - Total Verified Charging Stations: 166 stations
  - Supervised Training Matrix: 154 fully labeled stations
  - Class Distribution:
      * Class 1 (DC Fast, >= 50 kW): 131 stations (85.06%)
      * Class 0 (AC Slow / Non-Fast, < 50 kW): 23 stations (14.94%)
      * Imbalance Ratio: 5.70 to 1
  - Held-out Unknown Target: 12 stations (excluded from training)
  - Total Attributes: 78 raw and engineered attributes

4.2 Primary Data Sources
  - OpenChargeMap (OCM) & OpenStreetMap (OSM): Geographic coordinates, charger 
    specifications, road network topology, and Points of Interest (POIs).
  - VAHAN Dashboard (MoRTH): Multi-year district-wise vehicle registration records 
    (FY 2018 through FY 2024).
  - TGSPDCL DISCOM: 288 standardized commercial electricity billing records across 
    operational distribution circles.
  - Census of India & Telangana Socio-Economic Outlook: District population density, 
    urbanization percentages, and geographic land area.

4.3 Data Integrity & Anti-Fabrication Principles
  - Road Traffic Density: Public traffic volume (AADT) is 100% missing across Telangana 
    rural and urban road segments. In accordance with scientific integrity, traffic density 
    was completely excluded from the predictor matrix rather than fabricated via imputation.
  - Postal Pincodes: Retained strictly as human-readable metadata; never used as a 
    predictive feature due to non-uniform boundary definitions.
  - DISCOM Billing Data: Preserved at section/month administrative resolution for 
    macro-planning; never artificially disaggregated into synthetic station-level plug loads.


SECTION 5.0: FEATURE ENGINEERING & THE LEAKAGE QUARANTINE GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 The 21 Certified Exogenous Features
The predictive engine operates exclusively on 21 verified exogenous variables grouped 
into three functional stacks:

Stack 1: Spatial & GIS Connectivity (8 Features)
  1. latitude: Geographic latitude coordinate
  2. longitude: Geographic longitude coordinate
  3. distance_to_highway_km: Orthodromic distance to nearest National/State Highway
  4. distance_to_city_center_km: Distance to nearest municipal administrative center
  5. nearby_poi_count: Total commercial/amenity POIs within a 1 km radius
  6. highway_proximity_score: Inverted, bounded proximity metric to highway corridors
  7. city_center_proximity_score: Inverted, bounded proximity metric to city centers
  8. poi_diversity_index: Shannon Entropy quantifying commercial amenity mix

Stack 2: Physical Road Network Topology (7 One-Hot Encoded Features)
  9. road_type_motorway: Access-controlled high-speed corridor indicator
 10. road_type_primary: Primary arterial trunk road indicator
 11. road_type_secondary: Inter-district connector road indicator
 12. road_type_residential: Local urban/suburban street indicator
 13. road_type_service: Commercial service/fuel station access lane indicator
 14. road_type_trunk: Heavy freight transit corridor indicator
 15. road_type_unclassified: Minor connector route indicator

Stack 3: District Demographic & Regional Context (6 Features)
 16. district_population_density_2011: Census baseline density (persons per sq. km)
 17. district_urban_population_pct: Percentage of population in urban settlements
 18. district_projected_density_fy24: Compound demographic growth projection
 19. district_nh_crossing_count: Count of intersecting National Highway corridors
 20. district_ev_penetration_rate: Historical ratio of EVs to total registered vehicles
 21. discom_utility_data_available_flag: Indicator of formal electric utility coverage

5.2 The Strict Leakage Quarantine Gate
To guarantee valid generalization, an explicit filter quarantines and blocks all 
internal station hardware specifications from the model:
  - charging_power_kw: Direct mathematical leak of the target definition
  - charger_type: Direct string description revealing power tier
  - station_capacity: Physical kilowatt capacity directly indicates fast charging
  - connector_type: CCS-2 / CHAdeMO plugs directly reveal DC fast architecture
  - number_of_chargers: Physical installation count correlates directly with capital tier
  - operator: Private charging network identity reveals proprietary fast networks
  - district_fast_stations & district_fast_charger_ratio: Macro-level target leaks
  - station_id & database metadata: Non-generalizable database primary keys


SECTION 6.0: METHODOLOGY & THREE-TRACK ANALYTICAL SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 System Workflow Architecture
The system processes information through three distinct, non-overlapping analytical tracks:

  Track A: Observed Station Infrastructure Classification (Micro-Level)
    - Granularity: Individual Physical Station Location
    - Input Matrix: 21 Certified Exogenous Features
    - Machine Learning Model: Tuned Random Forest (Threshold tau = 0.76)
    - Outputs: Predicted Infrastructure Class (DC Fast vs AC Slow) + TreeSHAP Waterfall 
      Attributions + Actionable Counterfactual Site Recommendations

  Track B: Regional EV Adoption Dynamics (Macro-Level)
    - Granularity: District x Financial Year (FY18 - FY24)
    - Data Origin: MoRTH VAHAN Registration Registry
    - Metrics: Historical EV Stock, Growth Rates, and Infrastructure Gap Index
    - Role: Quantifies current and projected local charging demand pressure

  Track C: Electric Utility DISCOM Context (Macro-Level)
    - Granularity: Utility Section x Billing Month (FY23 - FY24)
    - Data Origin: TGSPDCL Commercial Billing Archives
    - Metrics: Section Billed Units (kWh), Revenue Demand, Commercial Tariffs
    - Role: Evaluates regional distribution grid readiness and power throughput capacity

  Downstream Synthesis:
    Tracks B and C feed directly into the AHP-TOPSIS Multi-Criteria Decision Engine 
    alongside spatial connectivity indices, producing the Explainable Infrastructure 
    Priority Score (EIPS) for all 33 districts.


SECTION 7.0: EXPERIMENTAL SETUP, SPATIAL VALIDATION & MODEL SELECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Spatial Partitioning Protocol
Standard random train-test splitting causes massive data leakage in spatial systems 
due to spatial autocorrelation (nearby stations sharing unmeasured geographic characteristics). 
To enforce genuine generalization, a District-Grouped Spatial Partitioning protocol was used:
  - Development Set (80%): 119 stations across 25 administrative districts, reserved 
    strictly for cross-validation and hyperparameter tuning.
  - Independent Outer Test Set (20%): 35 stations across 8 completely held-out districts 
    (26 DC Fast, 9 AC Slow).
  - Spatial Overlap: Exactly 0 districts overlap between development and test sets.

7.2 Hyperparameter & Decision Threshold Search Space
On the development set, 5,256 model-threshold experiments were conducted:
  - Evaluated Algorithms: Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM
  - Total Model Configurations: 292 unique hyperparameter parameter grids
  - Evaluated Probability Thresholds: 18 distinct thresholds (tau = 0.10 to 0.95, step 0.05)
  - Validation Engine: 4-Fold GroupKFold using administrative district as grouping factor

7.3 Pre-Registered Selection Hierarchy
Model selection was governed strictly by development metrics in order of priority:
  1. Highest Mean Development GroupKFold Cross-Validation Accuracy
  2. Highest Mean Development Balanced Accuracy
  3. Highest Minority-Class F1-Score & Recall
  4. Highest Mean Precision-Recall Area Under Curve (PR-AUC)

SECTION 9.0: EXPLAINABLE AI (XAI) & ACTIONABLE COUNTERFACTUAL REASONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 TreeSHAP Local & Global Attributions
To convert black-box machine learning into defensible public planning decisions, 
exact TreeSHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is integrated:
  - Global Feature Importance: Evaluates the mean absolute Shapley values across 
    all 154 stations. Top decisive drivers include:
      1. highway_proximity_score (strongest positive driver for DC Fast)
      2. distance_to_highway_km (inverse correlation with fast charging viability)
      3. road_type_trunk & road_type_motorway
      4. nearby_poi_count (density of surrounding amenities)
      5. district_urban_population_pct
  - Local Waterfall Decompositions: For any evaluated station coordinate x, TreeSHAP 
    decomposes the model prediction into additive feature contributions:
      f(x) = phi_0 + SUM [ phi_j(x) ]
    where phi_0 is the base expected model value and phi_j is the Shapley contribution 
    of predictor j, showing exactly why a candidate qualified or failed.

9.2 Actionable Constrained Counterfactual Optimization
When a proposed site is classified as AC Slow (P(DC Fast) < 0.76), planners require 
actionable guidance on how to upgrade the site's viability. The framework formulates 
a constrained optimization search:
  - Objective: Find the minimum feasible perturbation delta across site attributes such that:
      P(DC Fast | x + delta) >= 0.76
  - Permissible Actionable Levers (Planners can alter):
      * distance_to_highway_km (relocating site closer to an arterial corridor)
      * distance_to_city_center_km (adjusting urban proximity)
      * nearby_poi_count (co-locating with commercial retail, dining, or fuel plazas)
      * poi_diversity_index (attracting mixed-use commercial amenities)
  - Strictly Forbidden Levers (Immutables):
      * Regional demographic metrics (district population density, EV penetration) 
        and fundamental road classifications cannot be modified for a single station site.


SECTION 10.0: MACRO STRATEGIC PLANNING LAYER (AHP-TOPSIS & EIPS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 Multi-Criteria Decision Framework
The Explainable Infrastructure Priority Score (EIPS) ranks all 33 districts of 
Telangana to guide equitable public budget allocation. EIPS operates downstream of 
machine learning, avoiding any circular feedback.

10.2 Criteria Weighting via Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)
A pairwise comparison matrix was constructed and solved for principal eigenvectors:
  - Criterion 1 (C1: Regional EV Adoption Rate): Weight = 0.2565
  - Criterion 2 (C2: Infrastructure Gap Index): Weight = 0.3449 (Highest Weight)
  - Criterion 3 (C3: National Highway Corridor Importance): Weight = 0.1329
  - Criterion 4 (C4: Commercial Grid Throughput Context): Weight = 0.1329
  - Criterion 5 (C5: Geospatial Land-Use & POI Suitability): Weight = 0.1329
  - Mathematical Consistency Ratio:
      CR = 0.000891 (Significantly lower than the 0.10 threshold, proving mathematical rigor)

10.3 Ranking via TOPSIS Distance Metric
Districts are evaluated against the positive ideal solution (A+) and negative ideal 
solution (A-) across normalized weighted criteria:
  - Relative Closeness: EIPS_i = S_i^- / (S_i^+ + S_i^-)
  - Range: EIPS spans between 0.0 (lowest priority) and 1.0 (highest priority).
  - Priority Tiers Identified:
      * Tier 1 High-Density Urgency: Hyderabad, Rangareddy, Medchal-Malkajgiri, 
        Warangal Urban, and Sangareddy (driven by extreme adoption combined with large supply gaps).
      * Tier 2 Highway Corridors: Nalgonda, Suryapet, and Mahabubnagar (driven by NH-65 
        and NH-44 inter-state transit freight corridors).
      * Tier 3 Rural Emerging: Adilabad, Nirmal, and Wanaparthy (requiring foundational 
        state equity charging programs).

SECTION 12.0: REALISTIC LIMITATIONS & SCIENTIFIC ROADMAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.1 Documented Academic Limitations
  1. Sample Size Constraints: Evaluated on 154 verified labeled stations across 33 
     districts; represents verified infrastructure rather than a total state census.
  2. Severe Target Imbalance: The 5.7:1 real-world ratio (131 DC Fast vs 23 AC Slow) 
     reflects commercial market bias, resulting in conservative minority-class recall (11.11%).
  3. Traffic Telemetry Absence: Real-time road traffic volume (AADT) is unavailable 
     in public domain records and was deliberately omitted to preserve data integrity.
  4. Utility Data Resolution: TGSPDCL billing records represent section/month aggregates, 
     serving as macro grid context rather than real-time transformer SCADA telemetry.

12.2 Future Roadmap for Performance Enhancement
Future improvements should be driven by Information Expansion rather than unprincipled 
overfitting on the existing test set:
  - Priority 1: Expanding statewide charging station registries to 500+ verified locations 
    in partnership with TSREDCO.
  - Priority 2: Targeted field cataloging of AC Slow and destination chargers to balance 
    the ground-truth class distribution.
  - Priority 3: Incorporating 11kV and 33kV distribution transformer headroom and feeder 
    capacity directly from utility SCADA networks.
  - Priority 4: Integrating physical NHAI highway toll plaza counts and sensor-derived AADT.
